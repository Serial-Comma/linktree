Final wiring: 4-wheel and tricycle

This is the wiring for both drive layouts, drawn in the same style as the conversion guide. The robot never turns: it drives straight to the wall, throws the ball, then reverses straight back. The 9V battery connects straight to Vin with jumpers because the board has no barrel jack. Only the motor leads differ between the two layouts.

Run sequence

Both motor channels always turn the same way. Each side has its own PWM pin for a trim value, which keeps the robot straight without steering.

Layout A

4-wheel drive

Top view. Channel A drives the left pair and Channel B drives the right pair.

  • Left pair (FL + RL): both red leads go in the OUT1 row (pin 3) and both black leads in the OUT2 row (pin 6).
  • Right pair (FR + RR): both red leads go in the OUT4 row (pin 14) and both black leads in the OUT3 row (pin 11).
  • Each channel carries two motors, so both channels share the load equally.
  • Trim starts at 0 on both sides. Only add trim if the drive test shows the robot pulling to one side.

Layout B

Tricycle: one front wheel, two rear

Top view. The front motor is the old front-right motor, moved to the centre. It stays on Channel B.

  • Rear-left (RL), on its own: red lead in OUT1 (pin 3), black lead in OUT2 (pin 6).
  • Front (F) + rear-right (RR): both red leads in OUT4 (pin 14), both black leads in OUT3 (pin 11).
  • If you flipped the front motor end-for-end when moving it to the centre, swap its two leads. Send r in debug mode: F and RR must both push forward.
  • Channel B carries two motors and channel A only one, so B's motors run slightly slower. That tends to pull the robot toward the right. Start with R_TRIM ≈ +8 and adjust after the drive test.

Test points (TP)

The green TP circles mark a breadboard hole left free in each motor-output row, so you can push a multimeter probe in without unplugging anything. Put the meter on DC volts and probe across a pair: OUT1 and OUT2 for channel A, OUT4 and OUT3 for channel B. The reading is the voltage the motors on that channel actually get.

Running the 9V into Vin

Feeding Vin directly is fine electrically. The Uno's regulator accepts 7–12 V on Vin, the same as it would get through the barrel jack. There is one difference to watch.

No reverse-polarity protection on Vin. On an Uno, the barrel jack feeds Vin through a protection diode, and that diode is what saves the board if the battery goes on backwards. Wiring straight into Vin skips it. With the leads reversed, even for a second, the regulator can fail and take the board with it.

  • Red to Vin and black to the GND next to it, every time. Mark the red jumper with a strip of tape.
  • Before plugging in, check the clip with a multimeter: +9 V on the lead going to Vin.
  • Optional insurance: put a 1N5819 Schottky diode in series with the red lead (band toward Vin). It costs about 0.3 V, which leaves roughly 8.7 V at Vin.
  • Crimp or solder the clip leads onto male jumper pins and tape them to the Arduino. A loose Vin wire on the bump resets the board mid-run.
  • Leaving USB plugged in while the 9V is connected is safe on an Uno, because the board switches between the two supplies automatically.

Why keep four signal wires if the robot never turns?

You could tie D4 to both IN2 and IN4 and use one direction pin. It saves one jumper, but it needs a link across the chip and makes debugging harder. The two PWM pins matter more. Two sides of cheap TT motors never spin at exactly the same speed, and with no steering the only way to hold a straight line is to slow the faster side slightly. In the tricycle, channel B also carries two motors to channel A's one. So the four-wire layout stays.

Keep the L293D #2 spare. If the chip cuts out on the ramp (it gets hot and shuts down when overloaded), piggy-back #2 on top of #1 (solder its legs onto #1's) to double the current each channel can supply.

Code changes

The pin map is unchanged, so the wiring works with the earlier sketch. The new sketch eg1311_final.ino adds:

  • TRICYCLE flag that picks the starting trim and the sensor stop offset.
  • L_TRIM / R_TRIM applied inside driveStraight() for both forward and reverse.
  • The run sequence from the top of this page: forward, stop at STOP_CM, throw, reverse for REVERSE_MS.
  • A new debug command t: a 2-second straight run for tuning trim. Mark the start line with tape and measure the drift.
The pin map matches the conversion guide. Diagrams show a genuine Uno's power-header order (5V, GND, GND, Vin).
